Hello, I'm Aman. I live in New Delhi, and whenever I visit a new city or even a neighborhood, there's a good chance I'd spend some time thinking about the environment around me — its footpaths, its bus routes, the layout of the space, the metro lines slowly stitching new neighborhoods together. I also find myself noticing how a local community comes together — whether there's a park bench where elders gather in the evening, or a stretch of street where kids end up playing after school, or any real opportunity for different generations to do something together, rather than just living side by side. I've lived in Indian cities my whole life, save for about three years away in New Jersey for graduate studies at the Bloustein School and the work that followed it. Even from there, I found myself comparing everything back to home — what worked, what didn't, and why.

I trained first as an engineer, writing software for banks and public institutions, before something shifted and I went back to study City and Regional Planning. I wanted to understand cities not just as systems to optimize, but as places where people connect with opportunity and get the chance to fulfill their potential. That question has stayed with me ever since: how do we use good data and thoughtful design to make our everyday surroundings kinder and safer, so people can happily move through their lives without the city constantly getting in the way.
